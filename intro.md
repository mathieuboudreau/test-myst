# Quantitative MRI Course

Welcome to this open-access, interactive course on **Quantitative MRI (qMRI)**.

This course is designed for students and researchers who want to build a
solid understanding of the physical principles, pulse sequences, signal
models, and fitting strategies used in quantitative MRI — from basic NMR
physics to advanced tissue mapping techniques.

## What is Quantitative MRI?

Conventional clinical MRI produces images whose pixel intensities are a
complex mixture of tissue properties, scanner hardware, and acquisition
parameters. In contrast, qMRI aims to produce **maps of physical tissue
properties** — such as longitudinal relaxation time (T1), transverse
relaxation time (T2), and the local RF transmit field (B1) — that are
reproducible across scanners and sites.

These quantitative maps have important applications in:

- **Neuroimaging**: detecting subtle white matter changes in neurological disease
- **Oncology**: characterizing tumour microenvironment
- **Cardiac imaging**: assessing myocardial fibrosis
- **Multi-centre studies**: enabling reliable data pooling across sites

## Course Structure

| Chapter | Topic |
|---------|-------|
| 1 | A Quick Primer of MRI Physics |
| 2 | B1 Mapping |
| 3 | T1 Mapping |
| 4 | T2 Mapping |
| 5 | Magnetization Transfer |

Each chapter begins with an introduction, covers three important
methods or techniques, and closes with a conclusion. Each technique
section contains four sub-sections: **Pulse Sequence**, **Mathematical
Model**, **Simulations**, and **Data Fitting**.

## How to Use This Book

- **Equations** are typeset with LaTeX and rendered in your browser.
- **Interactive figures** let you explore how signal depends on tissue
  and acquisition parameters — use the sliders and controls to build
  intuition.
- **Code cells** show the Python code used to generate each figure.
  Click the toggle to reveal or hide the source.
- **Citations** link to the primary literature. A full reference list
  appears at the end of each chapter.

## Prerequisites

Helpful background (not strictly required):

- Electromagnetism — static fields and oscillating RF fields
- Classical mechanics — angular momentum and precession
- Introductory calculus and linear algebra
